Project 1

fplwire

Millions play Fantasy Premier League, and every week they face the same homework: injuries, press conferences, price changes, fixtures. fplwire does the homework and shows the answer next to your own team.

  • Status Live at fplwire.com and app.fplwire.com
  • Articles Nearly 200 published automatically
  • Price Free, Pro at €6 a month

Tap a screen to play it.

My part

Everything. The idea, the product decisions, the design reviews, the legal pages, the pricing, and shipping every change. Claude Code is the engineer. I'm the product manager: I set the direction, make the calls and check the work.

How AI fits

Claude writes the articles from the same data the app uses, and writes a personal read of each Pro user's squad. Behind the scenes, AI agents build, test and run the whole thing.

A product decision

No cookie banner, ever. Instead of adding one, I removed the reason for it: visitors are counted without cookies or third-party scripts, and per-visit records are deleted after two days.

What I learned

Correctness beats polish. When the app said a used chip was still available, the fix was checked against official data for all 554 players, not eyeballed. Users forgive ugly. They don't forgive wrong.

Project 2

Ask Ehsan

Recruiters ask the same ten questions. So I built an assistant that answers them, any time, from my CV and project notes, and says so when it doesn't know.

  • Status Live at ask-ehsan.vercel.app, and in the terminal below
  • Built with Next.js, Claude, Vercel

My part

Wrote what it knows and what it must refuse, picked the starter questions, and tested it the way a recruiter would: on a phone, in a hurry.

How AI fits

Claude answers every question, grounded only in my profile. Off-topic questions get a polite no.

A product decision

Years of experience are calculated, not typed. On 1 January the number ticks up by itself, so the bot is never a year out of date.

What I learned

Every check needs a control. "It answered" proves nothing until an unrelated question is also tested and correctly declined.

Project 3

How I work with AI

Not a tool, a way of working. I run my side projects like a product team, where the team is AI agents and I'm the product lead.

  1. A daily loop. Every day an agent checks the product's health, picks one item from the backlog, ships it, and emails me a report.
  2. Fake users, real feedback. Synthetic personas (a casual player, a spreadsheet nerd, a first-timer) try the app and report what confused them.
  3. Checklists the agents can't skip. Legal (EU and Swedish consumer and privacy law), design at six screen sizes in light and dark, and "prove it before you claim it".
  4. Hard lines. Agents ship almost everything, but never contact people, move money, delete data, change legal pages or add costs without me.

A product decision

Speed is cheap now; judgement isn't. So the system is built around where I say yes, not around writing code.

What I learned

A sent email isn't a delivered email. Most AI mistakes aren't bad code but unchecked claims, so every "done" ends with a checklist of what was and wasn't verified.
